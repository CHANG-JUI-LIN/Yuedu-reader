The lamps along the harbour wall came on one by one, and the boats that had been out since dawn turned for home with their lights low on the water. A dog barked somewhere behind the fish market and was answered by another.

Nobody on the quay paid them any attention. The day's catch was already sold, the ice was already melting in the gutters, and the men were thinking about supper.

At the end of the pier an old woman sat on an upturned crate and mended a net by the last of the light, her fingers moving faster than anyone could follow. She did not look up when the boats came in.
